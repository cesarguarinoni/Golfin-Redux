## 2d. Second review round (2026-09-05) — three more

**A. The target grew back during the up-flick.** `ResetSwing` ran `RedrawBands()` immediately
after the commit, so the bands snapped to full width in the same frame as the flick — the player
watched the target they had just narrowed pop open as they released. The bands now stay narrow
through the flick and the resolve, and re-open on the NEXT pointer-down (which already reapplies
the layout at zero power). `TheBandsReopen_ForTheNextSwing` was rewritten to assert that, rather
than the old behaviour it had been locking in.

**B. The band colours did not match Figma — and neither did the lane or the track.** Not a wrong
colour: **Figma composites in sRGB and Unity blends in LINEAR**, so every translucent element came
out too light. Measured against `reference/pendulum_timing.png`:

| element | Figma | built (node alphas) | delta | built (fixed) | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lane fill | (100,100,100) | (128,151,117) | +28 +51 +17 | see note | −3 −6 +3 |
| lane stroke | (163,173,163) | (192,204,190) | +29 +31 +27 | — | −3 −6 +5 |
| track | (19,49,55) | (43,71,58) | +24 +22 +3 | (19,47,55) | **0 −2 0** |
| BandGood | (196,188,138) | (224,208,148) | +28 +20 +10 | (196,186,137) | **0 −2 −1** |
| BandJust | (175,230,170) | (178,231,169) | +3 +1 −1 | (175,228,168) | **0 −2 −2** |
| centre pip | (255,59,59) | (250,56,49) | −5 −3 −10 | (248,59,53) | −7 0 −6 |

The fix per element depends on what it sits on:

- **The two bands sit on the TRACK — a parent whose colour is known — so they are pre-composited
  OPAQUE** at the reference render's own pixels. Exact, backdrop-independent, and now assertable:
  `colour.band_good` / `colour.band_just` / `colour.centre_pip` read the tint back off the live
  `Image` and compare to the node value.
- **The track's fill is pre-composited opaque too**, because one alpha could not fit all three
  channels (solving gave .915/.859/.699 — navy and grass are too far apart in blue).
- **The lane stays translucent** (it is a veil over turf and should be), with its alpha SOLVED:
  `a = (lin(target) − lin(backdrop)) / (lin(colour) − lin(backdrop))`. The first solve was fitted
  to the reference render's darker backdrop (.101) and then read +15/+10/+28 too light over real
  fairway; refitted to turf — the backdrop it actually sits on, always — it is .05, landing within
  (−3,−6,+3) of what Figma's own 14 % would draw over the same grass. The stroke's .365 solve holds
  over both, because the linear residual shrinks as alpha rises.

The one-alpha-cannot-fit-every-backdrop limit is real and stated rather than papered over: a
translucent element fitted to one backdrop drifts on another. That is why the two elements that
could be pre-composited were.

**C. The 100 % / 120 % lines had not moved with the longer pill.** They were still drawn at the raw
pull distance, which after the pill grew left them floating in the upper half with an inert stretch
below. Three things now hold together:

- A tick is drawn at `HandleRestBelowBall + PendulumPull*Px` — i.e. **where the club head lands at
  that power**, not the raw pull distance. The club's pivot moved to its CENTRE so it has a
  reference point to land ON; with the old top-edge pivot the sprite hung a full 100 px below every
  line it was supposed to be sitting on.
- The lines still mean 100 % / 120 %, which required the pull to get longer: `PendulumPull100Px`
  300 → **380**, `PendulumPull120Px` 360 → **456**. A line only moves down honestly if the pull it
  represents does.
- **The lane derives its own height** (`deepest tick + club half-height + slack`) instead of being
  authored, so the pill can never drift from its own lines again. Result: **596 tall, ticks at
  76 % and 88 % down** — the node's proportions are 75 % and 90 % — with the club contained at full
  pull with 20 px to spare.

Gated by `geom.tick100_marks_100pc_pull`, `geom.tick120_marks_120pc_pull`,
`geom.ticks_sit_low_in_the_pill`, `geom.lane_contains_the_club_at_full_pull`. The pull thresholds
in those assertions come from the driver's own config, never re-derived from the ticks — that would
have been a tautology dressed as a test. Every hard-coded pull distance in the tests, the
acceptance bot and the video runner is now derived from config for the same reason: the video's
first re-record silently shot a "full power" swing at 76 % because a literal 300 had stopped
meaning 100 %.

## 3. The bug the real-entry rule found

**Every EditMode test passed and the scheme still did not work.** Driven through a real
`IPointerDown/IDrag/IPointerUp` gesture on a real hole, no shot fired at all.

The up-flick travels back **past the touch origin**, so by `OnPointerUp` the live `pullPx` is 0 and
the live power is 0 — the driver saw `power <= 0.02` and cancelled the swing silently. The player
pulls to 100 %, flicks clean, and nothing happens. The EditMode tests missed it because they
released with a single sample instead of dragging through the upswing.

Flick has always solved this: `ClubHandleDragger` commits `_peakPower`, not the live value. The
driver now does the same, and two things are latched:

- **`_peakPower` / `_peakCurve`** — the deepest point of the pull is the shot.
- **`_markerAtLatch`** — the marker freezes at the **upswing reversal** (`ShotController.IsAimLocked`),
  not at release. This is a deliberate deviation from SPEC § 3.2's literal "grade at pointer-up",
  and it is the identical argument `_timingAtLatch` already makes for the flick's arrow
  (`shot_timing_power` F15 D1): the thumb takes 50–150 ms to leave the glass, which at 1.8 Hz is
  ~0.2 of marker travel — a whole band. Grading at release would cost the player their JUST.

Three regression tests were added for it: `FlickUp_PastTheTouchOrigin_StillFires`,
`PeakPower_SurvivesAPartialRelease`, `MarkerFreezes_AtTheUpswingReversal_NotAtRelease`.

### Art: three PNGs, and why

SPEC § 4 says "Art: none new". Five of the seven shapes hold to that — the ticks, both bands, the
pip and both marker discs are `S_PillStadium` tinted, with `pixelsPerUnitMultiplier = 88 / radius`
so the caps come out at the node's radius instead of collapsing into an oval.

The **lane and the track could not**. Both are a translucent fill inside a translucent stroke, and
`S_PillStadium` is a *solid* stadium — the stroke layer paints the whole shape and a 14 %-alpha fill
on top cannot hide it. The first build made the lane a ~57 %-white slab and the navy track grey. They
are now one baked sprite each, generated from the node's own tokens by
`Docs/Scripts/make_pendulum_sprites.py` (the `UI_ELEMENT_PALETTE` baked-from-tokens convention:
edit the script, never the PNG), 9-sliced at border = radius so the stroke stays 2/3 px at any
size. The dashed ball ghost is a Figma node export for the same reason — a dashed ring is not a
stadium.
